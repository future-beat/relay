## 2. Cross-phase seams

Each seam traced end to end, not assumed.

**Phase 2 `to_thread` vs Phase 5 per-event writes vs Phase 6 `/metrics` — WIRED, one leak.**
`RunRecorder.record` and `execute_and_record` both run inside `asyncio.to_thread` (`agent.py:248, 377`), and the write-tier variant nests its `run_events` insert inside the tool's own transaction via `Database.transaction()`'s SAVEPOINT support — atomicity across two phases that were built months apart, and it holds. `/metrics` and `/runs/{uid}` each take exactly one offloaded read. The one place the discipline breaks is F-1 below.

**Phase 1 `_gate` vs Phase 5/6 buckets — WIRED.**
Every `_gate(bucket)` call site resolves to a `(bucket, tier)` key present in `_LIMIT_SETTINGS`: `create`, `read`, `process`, `process/outage`, `auth/anon`, and the two added later — `events/anon`, `run_detail/anon`. A missing pair would be a `KeyError` → 500 at `_limit_item`; there are none. The `public=True` branch deliberately skips the shared `auth` bucket, with the reason recorded (charging it made 60 drill-down opens kill the visitor's own feed, since EventSource treats non-200 as terminal). That is a seam someone actually walked.

**Phase 3 retrieval vs Phase 5 `run_events` vs Phase 6 cited-vs-not — WIRED, and defended.**
`normalise_citation` lives in `retrieval.py` with a docstring naming its exactly two callers: the guard (`agent._execute_guarded`) and the view of the guard (`events.project_run_detail`). The view compares `doc` + `id` + `anchors` — the same licensing set the guard accepts (`events.py:953-961`) — so the drill-down cannot say "cited nothing" about a citation the guard accepted. This is the single most likely place for a silent contradiction and it was closed on purpose.

**Phase 2 shutdown drain vs Phase 5 broker vs Phase 6 `/events` viewers — WIRED.**
Viewers are deliberately *not* registered in `RunRegistry` (a watching browser is not work to drain), `broker.close()` sits between the drain and `conn.close()`, and the sentinel wakes every open generator. The idle ceiling never renews on keep-alives (`main.py:774`) — the one bug that would have silently defeated `min_machines_running=0` is explicitly guarded and commented.

**`mcp_server.py` after four frozen phases — WORKS, with two caveats.** I ran it rather than reading it:
- `create_server(db)` constructs against the installed `mcp 2.0.0`; `Server(on_list_tools=…, on_call_tool=…)`, `types.Tool(input_schema=…)` and `CallToolResult(is_error=…)` all match the installed API.
- `build_mcp_registry` → `lookup_customer` returns the post-Phase-6 payload (no `recent_tickets.subject`); `search_docs` returns the Phase-3 shape with `anchors`; `_create_ticket`/`_list_open_tickets` still work against `Database` (they use `execute`/`commit`/`lastrowid`, all of which `Database` and `Result` expose).
- `tickets.origin` is not set by the MCP `create_ticket` → NULL → reads as *not* demo → the drill-down redacts. Fail-closed, correct by accident but correct.
- Caveats: **F-2** (dependency floor) and **F-4** (blocking DB on the MCP process's own loop).

## 5. Backward compatibility

- **SSE contract — compatible, additively extended.** `text`, `tool_use`, `tool_result`, `usage`, `resolution`, `error` all keep their shapes; `guardrail` and `notice` are new types, and `done` closes the stream. A v1 consumer filtering on type is unaffected. The `run_uid` was deliberately put on a *response header* rather than a new `run` frame precisely to avoid touching the contract — a considered decision, written down at `main.py:317-324`.
- **MCP surface — compatible, with one narrowing.** Same seven tools, same names, same tiers. `send_reply` gained an optional `citations` array (additive, defaulted). `lookup_customer`'s `recent_tickets` **dropped the `subject` field** — a deliberate disclosure fix (a stranger's typed words were accumulating on the seeded demo personas and being republished on a keyless route), reasoned about at length in `tools.py:48-59`. Considered, not accidental, and the right call; but it *is* a payload narrowing on the MCP surface and no MCP consumer was consulted because there are none.

## 6. Findings

**F-1 — WARNING — `record_run` runs synchronously on the event loop.**
`main.py:457`, inside `event_stream`'s `finally`. Every other DB touch in the codebase is offloaded, and `main.py:169-176` documents a *measured 0.81s loop stall* from exactly this pattern against a 3s container HEALTHCHECK. Phase 5 raised the contention this collides with (~10 `run_events` transactions per run, each taking `Database`'s process-wide `RLock` from a worker thread) and flagged it as `05-REVIEW IN-04` — "a headroom note, not a defect… worth measuring before Phase 6 raises concurrency." Phase 6 raised concurrency (a polled `/metrics`, a held-open `/events`, drill-down reads) and nobody went back and measured. Fix is one `await asyncio.to_thread(...)`; the reason not to do it blind is that this call sits in a `finally` reached during cancellation, where an `await` can be a different animal. Not user-visible at three runs of live traffic.

**F-2 — WARNING — `mcp>=1.2` floor cannot satisfy the code, and `create_server` has no test.**
`mcp_server.create_server` uses the `mcp` 2.x server API (`Server(on_list_tools=…, on_call_tool=…)`, `types.Tool(input_schema=…)`, `CallToolResult(is_error=…)`). Under `mcp` 1.x — which the `>=1.2` floor in `pyproject.toml` permits, with no lockfile to pin it — that is a `TypeError` at `create_server` and a field-name mismatch on `Tool`. `tests/test_mcp.py` covers `build_mcp_registry`, `list_mcp_tools` and `call_mcp_tool` but never `create_server`, so the suite is green on any `mcp` version. This is precisely the "frozen for four phases" rot the audit was asked to look for — it just came from *outside* the codebase rather than inside it. Fix: floor to `mcp>=2,<3` and add one construction test.

**F-3 — WARNING — SEC-04's counter does not exist.** See the requirements table. The requirement's wording promises an increment; the implementation provides a log line and a span attribute. Either wire a counter (the `run_events` guardrail rows already make it a `SELECT COUNT`) or amend the requirement text before archiving, so the milestone record does not claim something the code does not do.

**F-4 — WARNING — the MCP process does blocking DB work on its own event loop.** `_on_call_tool` calls `call_mcp_tool` → `_execute_guarded` → `Database` directly, with no `to_thread`. Phase 2's whole seam was placed in `agent.py`, and the MCP entry point never got it. Low impact (separate process, one client, stdio), but it means the two entry points no longer share a concurrency story — and both can hold the *same SQLite file* (`main.py:397` names cross-process "database is locked" as a real path).

**F-5 — NOTE — `.planning/STATE.md` is internally inconsistent.** Front matter says `status: verifying`, `completed_plans: 23/30`, `percent: 77`, while the body says "Milestone complete — 22/22 requirements, 6/6 phases"; "Current focus" still reads Phase 05. Bookkeeping only, but this is the file the next milestone bootstraps from.

## 7. Does the deferred set undermine the intent?

**No — and the set is unusually well-chosen.** Taken together (Phase 5's WR-03/08/11/12 and W-3; Phase 6's WR-01/WR-04 and WR-03's substance — `/metrics` without a perimeter and 3 of 6 queries full-scanning `runs`; NF-2 unexercised; the literal mask as a floor; grep-level front-end assertions), the deferrals share one property: none of them sits on the path between a visitor and the thing the milestone promises them. The intent is *credible, safe, observably-real, cheap*, and each of those survives:

- **Credible / observably-real** — untouched. The drill-down, the live feed, the citation highlighting and the charts all work in production; I read a real run's fifteen steps off the live service.
- **Safe** — the deferrals are honest about their own limits, which is what makes them acceptable rather than papered over. The literal mask is documented *in the code that implements it* as a floor that paraphrase defeats, in a docstring that explicitly refuses to claim otherwise (`main.py:548-551`). That is the correct posture for a demo whose disclosure surface is four fictional personas and the visitor's own words. NF-2 being unexercised is a real hole, but an unexercised path is not an open one.
- **Cheap** — `/metrics` lacking a perimeter is the one deferral with teeth, because it is the only unmetered public route that runs SQL, and three of its six queries full-scan a table that grows for the life of the Fly volume. At present traffic (3 runs, ever) this is free. It becomes the first thing that breaks if the demo is ever actually linked somewhere busy, and it is where I would spend the first hour of v1.1.

The one thing I would push back on: **grep-level front-end assertions plus no `create_server` test (F-2) means two of the four E2E entry points are verified only by reading.** The dashboard is saved by the CI container smoke test, which greps the *served* page for markup only the shipped template has — that is a genuine end-to-end check and it is why I am not raising the front-end gap higher. The MCP path has no equivalent, and that is the asymmetry worth closing.
